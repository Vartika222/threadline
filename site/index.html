Every tabin itsplace

Threadline is a Chrome extension that files every new tab into a named group by what it's about. Study, work, the job you're applying for. It runs entirely on your computer.

Filed by instinct

Fox squirrels bury their nuts grouped by kind, so they can find them again months later. Researchers call it chunking. Threadline does the same for your tabs: every page is filed the moment it loads, so the thing you opened an hour ago is where you'd look for it.

Delgado & Jacobs, "Caching for where and what", Royal Society Open Science, 2017.

How it decides

Your word first.

If you've ever moved a site into a group, that's where it goes. YouTube is remembered per channel.

Then what it knows.

Common sites are on a list. Job pages are spotted by their address, whatever the company does.

Then it reads.

A small language model compares the page with a description of every group and picks the closest. With Gemini Nano on, a fast model learns its answers and sorts in milliseconds.

It learns from you

Drag a tab into another group once, or pick a group for it in the popup, and every tab from that site goes there from now on. No settings page, no rules to write.

Nothing leaves your browser

  • The model ships inside it.The 23 MB language model is part of the extension, so sorting works offline.
  • No account, no server.Your tabs, their text and your corrections stay in Chrome's own storage on your computer.
  • No analytics.Nothing is counted or reported, anywhere.
  • Gemini Nano is local too.It's the model built into Chrome. The fast model that learns from it trains on your computer.
